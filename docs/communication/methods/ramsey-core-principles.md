## What it teaches (paraphrased)

1. **The core is short and old:** spend less than you make; give every peso a name; avoid debt.
   The message hasn't changed in decades and that is the point.
2. **Plan on paper before the month starts.** Budgeting is behavior more than math.
3. **Debt is a sales pitch that got easier.** Payments at the checkout made borrowing for food and
   cars invisible. Convenience is what makes it feel normal.
4. **Things that lose value eat net worth.** Tying up a big part of what you own in something that
   depreciates fast is a slow leak.
5. **Anger is the turning point:** drawing a line against the debt mechanism. The call is to act,
   not to feel guilty.
6. **Doing it differently for a while is the price.** Living unlike everyone else now is how you
   live differently later.
7. **Proven beats flashy.** The newest thing (coin of the month, points and miles) keeps arriving.
   Longevity and a track record are the trust signal.
8. **Simple isn't easy.** The most valuable ideas are easy to understand and hard to carry out; if
   something sounds boring, lean in.
9. **Change the delivery, not the message.** Any new medium, same content.

## Argentine rework

- **Cuotas sin interés, billeteras, pago en el checkout:** the local version of "credit at the
  grocery store". Name them with their real words; "sin interés" isn't free when prices assume it.
- **Depreciation:** cars behave differently in a high-inflation economy where prices track the
  dólar. Don't import the US claim; explain the idea, test it locally before quoting.
- **Anger target:** the structure (cuotas, interest, inflation), not the user.

## What we do NOT copy

- Religious framing, "weirdo" branding, political asides and the app plug.
- Moralizing on debt ("servant to the lender"): conflicts with the no-shaming rule.
- The "I've said the same thing for 35 years" authority argument as our claim. We're new.
