## What it teaches (paraphrased)

1. **Every peso gets a job.** Money with no assignment drips away without you noticing.
2. **Where you stand decides where the next peso goes.** Same lump sum, different destination
   depending on the step: first cancel the small debt left, then size the emergency fund, then
   check the retirement percentage, then the house. *The step you're on is the routing rule.*
3. **Sizing the emergency fund:** essential monthly expenses × 3 to 6, in a separate, accessible
   account that pays little. It's insurance, not an investment.
4. **Diagnose before prescribing.** The caller had already done half the steps; the advice was
   to tick what's done and act on what's missing.
5. **Risk is a ratio to your whole wealth.** The same possible loss is unbearable if it's all you
   have and tolerable if it's a small slice of a large net worth.
6. **Time horizon matters:** money you'll need in a few years shouldn't ride volatile assets.
7. **Generosity built in,** as a habit at the top of the path, not an afterthought.

## Argentine rework

- The example instrument ("money market at 1%") has no direct peso analog: name caja de ahorro,
  FCI de liquidez, plazo fijo, dólar billete, with liquidity and inflation caveats.
- "Fund in a low-return account" assumes stable currency. In pesos it loses value every month:
  the fund needs a currency decision (see [ramsey-baby-steps.md](ramsey-baby-steps.md)).
- No 401(k)/match. The fund-vs-invest ratio idea travels; the accounts don't.

## What we do NOT copy

- The praise-as-status tone ("well done, millionaire"). It ranks people by wealth; we don't.
- US figures, fund history and retirement balances.
- The fund's long-run average as if it were a promise.
