## What it teaches (paraphrased)

1. **Compounding feels wrong** because intuition is linear. The short view hides it, and in
   Argentina the short view is the default.
2. **Measure it in time, not percent.** The first chunk of savings takes the longest and every
   equal chunk after it takes less. The first 100k is far more than "10% of a million" in time.
3. **Three levers:** earn a bit more each year, save a big share of what you earn, invest the
   savings at a market-like return. The numbers in the example are illustrations.
4. **No investing, no compounding.** Money in a box takes the same time for every equal chunk.
5. **Saving means decoupling lifestyle from income.** Each raise gets eaten by an upgrade.
6. **Three families of investments:** owning companies, owning property, lending (bonds).
7. **Extreme saving is possible but costs wellbeing;** most people are better off with a
   sustainable rate.
8. **You only see the curve if you measure.** Flat early years look identical to failure, which
   is the perseverance-vs-obstinacy question again.
