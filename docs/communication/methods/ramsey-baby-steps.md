## What it teaches (paraphrased)

The problem it solves: people know the principles (budget, avoid debt, save, invest) but not
**what to do first**, so they freeze. Ramsey's answer is a fixed order:

1. Starter emergency fund (small buffer; fast, ~30 days, sell things if needed).
2. Pay off all debt except the house, smallest to largest (**snowball**), minimums on the rest.
3. Full emergency fund: 3–6 months of expenses ("we're the bank now").
4. Invest 15% of income for retirement (match first, tax-advantaged accounts first).
5. Save for kids' college.
6. Pay off the house.
7. Build wealth and give.

Other ideas worth keeping:
- **Intensity, then intentionality:** steps 1–3 are all-in ("gazelle intensity"); steps 4–7
  are steady and on purpose.
- **Order matters more than optimization:** an emergency fund is easier with no payments, so
  debt comes before the big fund.
- **Behavior over math:** snowball wins on motivation even when avalanche wins on interest.
- **A path beats paralysis:** one next step at a time.

## Argentine rework (what changes)

- **US$1.000 starter** → pesos vs. dólares, liquidity in 24 h (caja de ahorro, billetera,
  dólar billete). Already partly in 3.1.
- **Debt:** consumer rates in Argentina can far exceed the US; cuotas "sin interés" aren't
  free. Avalanche is often the math answer; snowball the behavior answer. Keep both.
- **401(k)/Roth/match don't exist** → FCI, plazo fijo UVA, CEDEARs, dólar MEP, bonos. Name
  the local kit instead.
- **Retirement:** ANSES probably not enough (already in 5.2); no 401(k) equivalent, so the
  "fondo de retiro" is a personal portfolio.
- **Inflation cycles** change what "safe" means; a peso emergency fund loses value.

## What we do NOT copy

- The name "Baby Steps", the 7-step framing, and "Total Money Makeover" as our own.
- The tone of the talk (moralizing on debt) — conflicts with our no-shaming rule.
- US-specific advice (401k match, Roth, college debt system).
- The "average baby-steps millionaire in ~10 years" claims; unverified, US-based, and a
  promise we don't make.
