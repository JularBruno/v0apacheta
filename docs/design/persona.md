# Ideal user — "Marcos"

One person we design for. When a decision is ambiguous, ask "does this work for Marcos?"

## Snapshot

- **24 years old** (range 18–35), lives in **Córdoba capital**, Argentina.
- **Studies + works** — university plus a part-time job or freelance work. May still live
  with his parents, may have just moved out with roommates, may have moved from another
  province.
- **Income is real but irregular.** Some months are tight, some are fine. Not poor, not
  comfortable. Ranges from "just the basics" to occasional good months.
- **Phone-first.** Lives on his phone, active across several social networks, uses
  **WhatsApp** as his main channel. Uses email only to sign up for things.
- Enjoys city life, looks for stimulation in his surroundings, sees friends on weekends.

## What he wants (the aspiration — this is what gets him in the door)

- Make his money grow. Beat inflación. Not fall behind.
- He's **curious about investing** — dólar, plazo fijo UVA, FCI, CEDEARs, crypto, "the
  financial revolution." He follows finance content.
- Values money for what it costs him to earn it. Understands, in principle, the power of
  compound growth. Not afraid to learn new things.

## What he actually needs first (the fundamentals — what the product delivers)

- He doesn't budget. He isn't sure what he spends. Small daily expenses pile up without
  him noticing.
- He may carry consumer debt — tarjeta, cuotas, a personal loan.
- He has tried before: an Excel sheet, a notes app, maybe another finance app. He dropped
  it within a week or two.

## How he feels about money

- **Overwhelmed** — there's too much information, it's hard to find trustworthy sources,
  and harder still to apply any of it day to day.
- **A little ashamed** he hasn't "figured it out" yet at his age.
- **Anxious** about the complexity and risk of serious investing — he can't picture how it
  turns into steady, sustainable returns.
- **Impatient** — he wants to get to the interesting part.

## What makes him bounce

- It feels like a **bank** — cold, bureaucratic, forms.
- It feels **preachy or moralizing** — lectures him about his spending or his debt.
- It feels like **homework** — heavy, text-dense, no momentum.
- It's **all basics with no visible path** to the wealth-building part. He needs to see
  the investing chapters waiting for him from day one.
- He believes he has **no time** to manage a portfolio or study finance.

## A day in his life

Marcos gets up, has breakfast with his family, drives to the facultad near his house.
Back home around midday, eats, rests briefly, then works in the afternoon before finishing
his day. Today he's more relaxed — it's Friday and the weekend with friends is close.

## The value we promise him

Show Marcos how small daily expenses accumulate without him noticing. Help him spot the
negative patterns, recognize them, and turn them into positive actions. Concrete example:
biking to the facultad for three months instead of driving, moving that fuel money into a
savings goal, then investing it. Small wins that are real wins.

## Design consequence

The **marketing and the top of the map speak to the aspiration** ("construí patrimonio
aunque la economía esté en contra"). The **product delivers the fundamentals first** — but
always renders the full trail ahead, so Marcos sees Capítulos 4–5 (investing, freedom)
waiting for him. Same move as Duolingo: you sign up to "speak Italian for the trip," it
teaches you with fundamentals and daily habit, and the path always shows what's next.
